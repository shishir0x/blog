# 🎓 The Failure I Became After Graduation

I always imagined that completing my bachelor's degree would feel like a victory. I thought the day I finished my **Bachelor in Computer Applications (BCA)** would be the day everything would finally start making sense. I thought I would have a clear direction, a good career path, confidence in myself, and a feeling that I had finally achieved something. 

But reality felt completely different. I completed my degree, but instead of feeling proud, I felt empty. I looked at myself and asked:

> **"After all these years of studying, what have I actually achieved?"**

And the answer scared me. I felt like I had a certificate in my hand but no clear identity attached to it.

---

## 🏃‍♂️ The Fear of Being Left Behind

One of the hardest feelings I have experienced recently is watching people my age move forward while I feel stuck in the same place. Everywhere I look, I see people achieving things:

✅ Someone is getting a great job.  
✅ Someone is moving abroad.  
✅ Someone is starting a business.  
✅ Someone is building a successful career.  
✅ Someone already knows exactly what they want from life.  

And then there is me. Still trying to understand myself. Still questioning my choices. Still wondering if I wasted my time. 

This is the worst part of comparison—it makes you feel like everyone else is living a life that you were supposed to have. I know social media only shows a small part of people's lives, but sometimes that doesn't stop the feeling of missing out. The feeling of:

> *"Everyone is moving ahead. Why am I still here?"*

---

## 🧭 A Degree Without Direction

When I started BCA, I thought technology was my future. I explored programming, web development, backend development, databases, cloud computing, and many other things. For a while, something would excite me. I would spend days learning about it. Then after some time, the excitement would disappear. I would start questioning everything:

- ❓ *"Is this really for me?"*
- ❓ *"What if I choose the wrong career?"*
- ❓ *"What if I spend years learning something that I don't even enjoy?"*

This constant searching made me feel even more lost. Instead of building one path, I kept looking for the perfect path. And maybe that was my biggest mistake. I was so afraid of choosing the wrong thing that I ended up not fully choosing anything.

---

## 😞 Feeling Like I Failed Myself

The hardest person to disappoint is not always other people. Sometimes it is yourself. 

I remember the dreams I had when I started college. I imagined becoming someone successful. Someone my family would be proud of. Someone who could look back and say, *"I made it."* But now, after completing my bachelor's degree, I sometimes feel like I am far away from that person.

It feels like I spent years preparing for a future that I am still unable to find. I feel like I should have achieved more, learned more, built more projects, and been more disciplined. I should have figured things out earlier. The list of "should haves" never ends.

---

## ⚖️ The Pressure of Expectations

There is also another kind of pressure that comes after graduation. People expect you to have answers. They ask:

💬 *"What are you doing now?"*  
💬 *"Where are you working?"*  
💬 *"What are your plans?"*  

And sometimes, the hardest part is not answering them. The hardest part is answering yourself, because deep inside, you are also asking the same questions. I want to make my family proud, become independent, and prove that all the struggles and sacrifices were worth it. But sometimes I feel like I am running out of time.

---

## 🌱 Maybe I Am Not A Failure

Maybe the biggest lie I have been telling myself is that I am a failure. Maybe I am not a failure. Maybe I am just someone who is still figuring things out. 

Life is not a race where everyone starts together and reaches the finish line at the same time. Some people find their path at 20, some find it at 30, and some people change careers multiple times before finding something meaningful. The fact that I am confused does not mean I am finished. It means I am searching.

---

## 🌅 Starting Again

I don't know exactly where I will be next year. I don't know whether I will become a developer, a data analyst, move into finance, continue studies abroad, or discover something completely different. 

But I know one thing: I cannot keep punishing myself for not becoming someone overnight. I need to stop looking at other people's timelines and start building my own. 

Maybe my journey is slower. Maybe I started late. Maybe I made mistakes. But mistakes are still part of the story. The person I want to become is not created by avoiding failure—it is created by continuing after failure.

---

> **Today, I may feel like I have achieved nothing. Today, I may feel behind. Today, I may feel lost. But this is not the end of my story. This is just the chapter where I am trying to find my way.**
